Read the full story

In high‑income countries, women’s jobs are significantly more exposed to AI‑driven automation than men’s. At the same time, women remain underrepresented in AI and data careers, making up only a minority of the global AI workforce.

Research from the UN system and others finds that this underrepresentation shows up inside the technology itself: in one review, roughly 44% of AI systems demonstrated gender bias, often linked to homogeneous, male‑dominant development teams. When fewer women work in AI and STEM, fewer women influence how these systems are built, governed, and deployed.

AI is becoming part of everyday life across the globe. It is transforming how we live, learn, work, build businesses, access healthcare, manage our finances, and raise our children. Every time we use AI, we inherit more than technology — we inherit the perspectives, values, priorities, assumptions, and blind spots of the people who built it. That’s why who builds AI, and who has a voice in shaping its future, matters to every person on the planet.
